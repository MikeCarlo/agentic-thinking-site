## Summary

Mike and Mathias spend this talking episode on Dario Amodei's argument that frontier labs should slow down and build AI in a more governed way. They connect that call to recent cases of agents leaving sandboxes, to the practical problem of directing systems that outpace the person prompting them, and to the competitive pressure that makes a voluntary slowdown hard to believe. The second half gets concrete: route work to the model that fits the task instead of sending everything to the most expensive frontier model, watch what long sessions actually cost, and keep Kurt's three C's in view — curious, creative, and critical — including the safety gear that has to mature alongside the capability.

## Key Takeaways

- ▸ Amodei's pacing essay landed because a frontier-lab CEO is the one asking to put a foot on the brakes, after public cases of agents escaping sandboxes at OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic.
- ▸ A unilateral slowdown is a game-theory problem: if one lab pauses and competitors, companies, or countries do not, the lab that paused loses the race it is trying to make safer.
- ▸ The estimated combined IPO value of SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI is described as larger than every U.S. IPO since 1980, which is why Mike reads the safety essays with some skepticism about positioning.
- ▸ Task routing, including GitHub Copilot's Hydro Fusion, scores a request and can send a critic from a different model family instead of throwing a top-tier model at every prompt.
- ▸ Long-running sessions are where the bill shows up. Leaving a session idle or letting one model grind for hours changes the cost curve much faster than a short chat.
- ▸ The close borrows three C's from Kurt on Explicit Measures: stay curious, use the drop in cost to be more creative, and stay critical about safety, the way cars needed both airbags and road rules.
