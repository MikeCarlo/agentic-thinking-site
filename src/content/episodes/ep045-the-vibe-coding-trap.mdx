## Summary

Mike and Mathias review a Medium article, The Vibe Coding Trap, about startups that ship an AI-generated MVP and then get into trouble. Mathias lists the article's examples: database corruption, missing indexes, wasted tokens, and security holes on a public site. Mike answers with a Microsoft demo that pitted a novice using AI against an expert, and with the recent shift at Tabular Editor and SQLBI toward agent harnesses. They disagree, productively, on whether a later model closes the trap. Mathias does not think the upfront architecture goes away. They end on the one failure an agent cannot patch after launch: a price or contract that was never going to be profitable, with OpenAI's $200 and $500 plans as the fresh example.

## Key Takeaways

- ▸ The article's trap is a product that looks shipped and then fails the unglamorous parts: indexes, token cost, and security. Mathias treats the stories as extremes, and still worth unpacking against their own builds.
- ▸ Mike's Microsoft demo is the split he keeps using. A novice with AI can look close to an expert. An expert with AI compounds, because they already know caching, capacity, and the API shape a high-volume app needs.
- ▸ Mathias asks the question the article leaves open: do better models erase the trap, or does a trained engineer stay necessary for something scalable and secure? He is skeptical that talking to a bot builds the next business with nobody holding it.
- ▸ Mike's counterexample is Tabular Editor and SQLBI. He heard them keep a human in the loop, then watched them ship agent tooling once Power BI's modeling MCP made a third-party modeler optional. A lot of "the AI failed" is a missing requirement.
- ▸ Mathias will hand an agent a component he does not care about, and stay hands-on for identity, authentication, and payments. If you cannot accept the AI getting that piece wrong, get closer to it.
- ▸ A crashed SQL database is usually recoverable: send the agent back with the failure. A business model priced too low for the tokens it burns is not. OpenAI pausing the $200 plan, launching a $500 plan, and cutting quotas on the $200 plan is the example they use, next to the June 26 Copilot pricing change.
