## Summary

Back from a short hiatus, Mike and Matthias spend the hour on Steve Yegge's mid-June Flat Curve Society article and the idea of a discernment horizon: modern models look interchangeable to most people even when labs claim a generation of gap, because the outputs have moved past what most of us can reliably judge. Mike connects that to restricted access on the strongest models and the practical problem of steering work you cannot review, while Matthias draws a line between everyday software that current models already crush and the research-grade systems that still need new frontier intelligence. They argue SaaS is back because cheap all-you-can-eat tokens are gone, one-shot replacements do not hold, and the real skill is routing work to cheaper models, building loops that validate instead of hand-holding, and treating agents like new engineers rather than magic. News woven through the conversation includes Grokbot, August releases from Chinese labs, OpenAI's discounted Luna as a default execution model, an eight-hour GitHub outage, Cursor Origin, and Entire as agent-first git hosts.

## Key Takeaways

- ▸ Yegge's discernment horizon says Opus-class and Fable-class outputs already feel the same to most people, even when the labs claim a full generation between them.
- ▸ The bottleneck is not more raw intelligence. It is token-efficient systems, long-horizon loops, and verification so an agent can finish a substantial task without constant hand-holding.
- ▸ SaaS is back because heavily subsidized tokens are being clamped and you still cannot one-shot anything non-trivial into a maintainable product.
- ▸ Route cheap work to cheap models. Matthias is using Luna as a default for well-specced research and implementation, and saving expensive models for planning and hard reviews.
- ▸ Do not spend tokens on `git push` when you are already at a terminal, and do not treat agents as skip-the-onboarding employees. They still need your process, musts, and review rules.
- ▸ GitHub's load and an eight-hour outage sit next to a wave of agent commits. Agent-first hosts like Cursor Origin and Entire are showing up because human-era CI was not built for that throughput.
